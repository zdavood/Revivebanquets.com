name: Website scope
description: Public website boundaries and verified capacity from the owner's optimization brief.
---
Revive website work is public-facing only. A separate agent owns VenueOS scheduling, inquiry processing, notifications, backend endpoints and data models. Preserve integration points and document required contracts rather than building a second CRM, form-storage system or scheduler.

**Why:** The owner explicitly divided these responsibilities in the marketing/conversion brief.

**How to apply:** Make safe frontend changes independently, but coordinate backend-dependent changes. Do not publish, change live domains or enable redirects without owner approval. Preserve authentic venue images and established gold/cream branding. Do not invent reviews, rental policies, inclusions or availability claims. Owner-confirmed seated capacity is up to 350 guests.

For VenueOS handoffs, never guess a tenant identifier or treat a generic calendar or HTTP 200 response as proof the integration works. Verify the public venue identity and distinguish unavailable configuration from unavailable dates.

**Why:** The owner's integration brief explains that missing tenant resolution previously appeared as “no openings,” and VenueOS development fixes may not yet be published.

**How to apply:** Keep production verification read-only: no test bookings, inquiries, customer emails or configuration changes. Check the hosted page identifies Revive and preserves the venue when switching options. Publication still requires separate approval.

Owner-confirmed venue facts: Revive is one indoor ballroom. The main banquet area is approximately 50 feet by 100 feet (about 5,000 square feet). Separate bar, bathroom and prep-kitchen areas are outside those dimensions. Never describe that figure as total venue size or use those separate areas to increase the 350-person seated capacity. Actual capacity depends on tables, dance floor and staging.

**Why:** The owner explicitly clarified the measurement's scope and capacity limits.

**How to apply:** Keep these distinctions in public copy, metadata, proposals and future layout descriptions.

Optional offerings confirmed by the owner: photo booth rentals, event staffing, catering through preferred partners, and hosted bar options. Availability and details belong in the event proposal; none are automatically included in base rental. No valet parking. No public prices, price ranges, dollar amounts, internal catalog labels, provider/vendor names, costs or staffing-source details.

**Why:** The owner supplied these business and disclosure rules and said the old website is being retired; stale claims from it must not be reused.

**How to apply:** Use customer-facing descriptions and actual Revive photos without implying pictured décor is included. The owner changed the VenueOS tour window to 14 days, but public availability must remain driven by the live scheduler, not a fixed marketing promise. Never invent an inquiry response-time commitment.

Keep inquiry response timing vague: the team responds fast, but the owner does not want to promise an exact time.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested general wording rather than a response-time commitment.

**How to apply:** Say the team will follow up about event details, availability and next steps. Do not add a numerical deadline or ask for one in future content updates.

Keep Revive's positioning broad rather than singling out South Asian or any other community.

**Why:** The owner rejected that emphasis in the hero and clarified that Revive should not focus on a particular community. This supersedes the earlier brief's community-specific examples.

**How to apply:** Use general event wording such as weddings, celebrations and corporate events. Preserve actual event photos without presenting Revive as a venue for a particular ethnicity.
